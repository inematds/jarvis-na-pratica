INTELECTO v2 is a free personal assistant built by INEMA that lives on your computer. It talks with you, remembers what you ask it to remember, and works with files, always with a safety lock. Here it comes ready to use under the nickname Jarvis (Iron Man's assistant), with a swappable brain: the AI powering it can be Claude Code or Codex, which you use through your existing Claude or ChatGPT subscription, or OpenRouter, an online AI marketplace that charges pennies per use. We gave all three the same conversation on the same machine and measured accuracy, time, and cost.

✅ This is for you if…

  • You have a computer or server for the assistant to live on. A phone cannot do this (it only runs Telegram). A server is a computer rented online that stays on all the time. Renting one usually costs a few dollars a month, depending on its size.
  • You have a paid Claude or ChatGPT plan, or are willing to add a few dollars of credit to OpenRouter.

Where we tested it: on one rented Linux server (4 processors, 15 GB of memory). We did not test it on smaller machines, or on your Windows PC or Mac. Claude Code and Codex work on Windows, Mac, and Linux, but the sandbox and Jarvis were only tested on Linux.

Never rented a server? Search for “VPS” (virtual private server) with any provider, choose Ubuntu (a Linux system), and write down the address (a number like 203.0.113.10), the username, and the password they give you.

How to connect: On Windows, open the Terminal app (or PowerShell). On Mac, open the Terminal app. Type ssh user@address (for example, ssh root@203.0.113.10), press Enter, and type your password. Now anything you type runs on the server. This remote terminal connection is called SSH.

How it works

The brain thinks. The rest belongs to Jarvis.

Think of a company: the brain is the employee you hire, and Jarvis is the office, with the rules on the wall. Replacing the employee does not change the rules.

You make a request→Jarvis adds memory and rules→the brain decides→the safety lock checks→it runs in a sandbox→answer

🧠 The brain only decides

It gets the conversation and says, “I want to check the disk” or “I want to delete the logs folder.” It cannot touch anything on the machine.

🔒 The safety lock belongs to Jarvis

It works like a gatekeeper for commands on the machine: read-only requests (check disk space, list folders) go straight through; any other request waits for your approval; anything irreversible (shut down or reboot the machine, delete everything, act as an administrator) is blocked even if you approve it. Files you ask Jarvis to create or edit always stay inside the work folder.

📦 The sandbox

Approved commands run in an offline sandbox locked to the work folder (a program called Docker creates this sandbox). If something goes wrong, it stays inside.

📒 Memory and logs

What you ask Jarvis to remember is stored by Jarvis, not by the AI. Every command, approved or not, is recorded in a log.

💬 What it’s like to use day to day

Through Telegram on your phone: send a message, get a reply, and if you ask it to change something, it asks, “Posso rodar este comando?” (May I run this command?) and waits for your /sim or /nao. You can also use the command screen on the machine itself (the terminal), in the same way. See Using it through Telegram.

⚠️ What this does not promise

It does not make any AI smarter: a weak brain will still give wrong answers. What it guarantees is protection: even if it makes a mistake, it cannot delete or reboot anything without going through the safety lock. We also ran one test round per brain on one server. Times vary from day to day.

Using it through Telegram

Talk to your Jarvis on your phone

Jarvis lives on a computer or server, and you talk to it through Telegram wherever you are. Only people on your owner list can talk to it. Everyone else is ignored.

1. Create your bot

In Telegram, open @BotFather (the official bot that creates bots), send /newbot, and choose a name. It gives you a long code: that is your bot’s key. Keep it safe and do not show it to anyone.

2. Ask your AI to connect it

Tell your AI (the same one you used in “Getting started”): “enable Telegram mode for Jarvis in Practice, but do not ask me for the token in the conversation.” It creates the configuration file and gives you a command like nano .env, which opens the file in the server terminal. Paste the key there (in the terminal, paste with Ctrl+Shift+V or right-click), save with Ctrl+O and Enter, exit with Ctrl+X, and it starts the service, which restarts on its own if the machine reboots.

Never paste the key into your conversation with the AI or any other chat: anyone with the key can control your bot.

3. Set yourself as the owner

Send /id to your bot. It replies with your Telegram number. The AI adds that number to the owner list. Until then, the bot will not answer anyone.

4. Chat and switch AIs whenever you want

Make your request as usual. Before changing anything, it asks and waits for /sim (yes) or /nao (no) (2 minutes). To see which AI is thinking: /cerebro. To switch: /cerebro claude, /cerebro codex, or /cerebro openrouter (this one charges per use). Your memory and safety lock stay the same.

What we proved: with a simulated Telegram, strangers are ignored, the owner gets through, deletion asks for approval, /nao keeps the folder, /sim deletes it, and a switched brain remembers what was said before. Our real test bot connected to the server and identified itself to Telegram (record in results); we have not yet tested a real conversation from a phone.

Common questions

Before you get started

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Paste the request, read the plan the AI shows you, and say yes or no. The AI does the installation.

How much does it cost?

The project is free. With Claude Code or Codex, use the subscription you already have. With OpenRouter, you pay only for usage, in pennies.

Do I need all three brains?

No. One is enough. Having three options lets you switch whenever you want.

Will it work on my Windows PC or Mac?

We tested it on a Linux server. In step 1 of “Getting started,” your AI checks your machine and tells you if it will work.

Could the AI delete something without me seeing?

Not through this setup. The brain does not run anything: Jarvis does, after the safety lock. Every command that does more than inspect something waits for your approval. Irreversible actions are blocked, and files it creates stay inside the work folder.

Does it work through Telegram?

Telegram mode is ready and has been tested this way: 5 tests with a simulated Telegram (strangers ignored, owner gets through, deletion asks for approval, /nao keeps the folder, /sim deletes it, and a switched brain remembers) and the real bot connected to our server and identified itself to Telegram. A real conversation from a phone is the next test. We measured all three brains through the terminal. See Using it through Telegram.

What if I already use INTELECTO?

This project does not change your INTELECTO. It only adds the three brains alongside it.
